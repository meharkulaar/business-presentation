Cancer by the numbers

What every family in Canada should know

Gentle transition out of the silence. "Let's look at what cancer means for families in Canada, in numbers. Behind every number is a person, so on these slides every leaf is a person."

About 2 in 5 Canadians are expected to be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime.

In 2026, that's an estimated 254,100 people in Canada, nearly 700 every day.

Sources: Canadian Cancer Society, Cancer statistics (Canadian Cancer Statistics 2025: 42% lifetime probability). 2026 projection: Brenner et al., “Projected estimates of cancer in Canada in 2026”, CMAJ, April 2026.

Click 1: five leaves appear. "Five leaves. Five people. Look at the four people sitting next to you." Click 2: two leaves turn red. "Statistically, two of the five of you will hear the word cancer." Click 3: headline. The 2025 report puts the lifetime probability at 42%, which is about 2 in 5. Click 4: "This year alone, about 254,100 people in Canada are expected to be diagnosed. That's nearly 700 people every single day." (254,100 ÷ 365 ≈ 696.)

Same disease. Two different outcomes.

Unprepared

Steven Hodges

  • Self-employed, wife and young child
  • Stage 4 throat and mouth cancer, 40 rounds of radiation plus chemotherapy
  • No disability insurance; his wife's plan didn't fully cover medication
  • Lost two years of steady income
  • About $125,000 in costs: emptied his RRSPs and TFSAs, took a second mortgage
Prepared

Sandra and Warren

  • Married couple with an infant daughter
  • Sandra was diagnosed with lymphoma
  • They had critical illness insurance
  • The coverage let Warren take care of Sandra and their daughter, and eased the financial and emotional strain
