## Slides

### 1. United Against Cancer

*Host · Welcome and reflection · 0 clicks · finish by 2:00*

Leaves fall on their own while guests are seated. When everyone is in, welcome the room: thank families for coming, and say that tonight is about something that touches all of us. Keep it warm and short.

### 2. Raise your hand if cancer has touched your family, or someone you love.

*Host · Welcome and reflection · 1 click · finish by 2:00*

Read the line slowly, then raise your own hand. Pause for hands and let people look around the room.

Click once: the text fades and the lavender ribbon appears. Lavender stands for all cancers. Hold 10 seconds of silence. Do not fill it.

### 3. Cancer by the numbers

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 0 clicks · finish by 5:00*

Gentle transition out of the silence. "Let's look at what cancer means for families in Canada, in numbers. Behind every number is a person, so on these slides every leaf is a person."

### 4. About 2 in 5 Canadians are expected to be diagnosed with cancer in their lifetime.

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 4 clicks · finish by 5:00*

Click 1: five leaves appear. "Five leaves. Five people. Look at the four people sitting next to you."

Click 2: two leaves turn red. "Statistically, two of the five of you will hear the word cancer."

Click 3: headline. The 2025 report puts the lifetime probability at 42%, which is about 2 in 5.

Click 4: "This year alone, about 254,100 people in Canada are expected to be diagnosed. That's nearly 700 people every single day." (254,100 ÷ 365 ≈ 696.)

> Sources: Canadian Cancer Society, Cancer statistics (Canadian Cancer Statistics 2025: 42% lifetime probability). 2026 projection: Brenner et al., “Projected estimates of cancer in Canada in 2026”, CMAJ, April 2026.

### 5. About 64% survive five years

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 2 clicks · finish by 5:00*

Shift to hope. Click 1: the number counts up. "Here's the good news. Most people survive. About 64% of Canadians live at least five years after a diagnosis, up from 55% in the early 1990s."

Click 2: let the question land. "Survival is the goal. But survival takes time, and time costs money."

> Source: Canadian Cancer Statistics 2021, Public Health Agency of Canada (about 64% five-year net survival; Canadian Cancer Statistics 2025 reports the same figure).

### 6. Nearly $33,000

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 2 clicks · finish by 5:00*

Click 1: the number counts up. "Nearly $33,000. That's the average cost to a patient, in out-of-pocket expenses and lost income."

Click 2: the chips appear. Name a few: "Medication that isn't covered. Parking at the hospital, week after week. Someone has to take time off to drive you. Home care. And the biggest one, lost income."

> Source: Canadian Cancer Society, Canadian Cancer Statistics report, December 2024.

### 7. What Canadians fear

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 3 clicks · finish by 5:00*

One stat per click. Pause after each one. These are working-age Canadians, ages 18 to 64, people like the ones in this room.

Click 1: retirement. Click 2: unpaid time off. Click 3: losing their job.

> Source: Canadian Cancer Society and Angus Reid Institute survey, February 2025.

### 8. How prepared are we?

*Host · Cancer by the numbers · 2 clicks · finish by 5:00*

Click 1: "Fewer than 1 in 10 Canadians, 9%, have critical illness insurance. More than half (57%) say they are worried about becoming seriously ill. We worry, but we don't plan."

Click 2: "And nearly 1 in 3 Canadians, 32%, have no emergency fund at all. For households earning under $100,000 it's 38%. 76% say the cost of living is what makes saving hard."

Then hand to the Foresters partner: "Our partner from Foresters Financial is here to share more."

> Sources: RBC Insurance poll conducted by Ipsos, 2,001 Canadians, August 2025. RBC Emergency Readiness Poll, July 2026.

### 9. Foresters Financial

*Foresters partner · Foresters Financial · 0 clicks · finish by 7:00*

The Foresters partner comes up and presents their own content. This slide stays up while they speak. When they finish, thank them by name and take the stage back.

### 10. Do you know exactly what would happen to your income if you couldn't work for six months?

*Host · The preparedness gap · 1 click · finish by 9:00*

Ask for a show of hands: "Who here knows exactly what would happen?" Let it be quiet. Most hands will stay down. That's the point, gently.

Click 1: read the line with compassion. No blame.

### 11. The challenges families face every day

*Host · Today's 8 challenges · 8 clicks · finish by 13:00*

One leaf per click, about 30 seconds each with a relatable example. Press A to show all eight at once if time is short.

1 High debt: mortgage, car loan, credit cards.

2 Lack of family time: two jobs, long commutes.

3 Limited income: one paycheque carrying a whole household.

4 Compromised lifestyle: saying no to the kids more than we'd like.

5 Lack of savings discipline: money left over at the end of the month rarely happens.

6 Lack of financial education: school never taught us this.

7 Insecure financial future: will we ever retire?

8 Losing time for wealth creation: every year we wait costs us.

Briefly mention that our philosophies, multi-handed income and 10% savings with the 3 rules and 3 goals of money, address these. Detail comes in Part 2.

### 12. Now add a cancer diagnosis.

*Host · Amplified 10x · 5 clicks · finish by 15:00*

Same eight leaves. "Now add a cancer diagnosis."

Clicks 2 to 5: one line each.
