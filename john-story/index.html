The secret weapon

The Capital Dividend Account (CDA)

A notional account. Not a bank account: a running tally of tax-free dollars in John’s corporation.
What fills it: the tax-free half of capital gains, capital dividends received, and the big one: life insurance paid to the corporation.
What it does: every dollar in it can be paid to John’s family as a tax-free capital dividend.
David tracks the balance and files the election. Mehar fills it.
A real case · proven in Tax Court

The Rogers family did exactly this.

$0Mthrough the CDA, tax-free

Corporate-owned life insurance on Ted Rogers, held in the family’s private companies.

On his passing, about $102 million was credited to the CDA and paid out as tax-free capital dividends.

The CRA challenged it. The Tax Court ruled for the family.

John doesn’t need $102 million. He needs the same playbook.

Rogers Enterprises (2015) Inc. v. The Queen, Tax Court of Canada.

How he borrowed

The Immediate Financing Arrangement (IFA)

1
Pay the premiumHis corporation pays $100K into the policy.
➜
2
Pledge the policyThe bank lends against the cash value, as collateral.
➜
3
Reinvest the cashThe loan funds clinic #2. Interest may be tax-deductible.
➜
4
Policy keeps growingUntouched, compounding, with the death benefit repaying the loan one day.

Walt Disney and Ray Kroc are widely reported to have used their policies the same way. One dollar, two jobs.

IFA structures must be set up with your accountant and lender. Interest deductibility depends on how the loan is used. Not tax advice.
